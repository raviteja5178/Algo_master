🏆 Data Verdict: ORB + Afternoon Momentum on Trending Days
SENSEX is sideways 57% of the time — most intraday strategies bleed on those days. The data shows exactly two high-probability windows:

1. Opening Range Breakout (09:15–09:35) — the first candle has an avg range of 280 pts, and on 68.6% of days price sustains a directional breakout from that range. This is the single best signal in the data.

What NOT to use: Any strategy that trades VWAP crosses on sideways days — sideways days average 9.1 VWAP crosses per day. That's pure whipsawing noise.

🔬 What the Data Says About Each Strategy

✅ ORB — Strongest Signal in the Data
ORB CE sustained on 34.7% of all days, ORB PE on 33.9%. Combined, 68.6% of days have a sustained directional ORB breakout. The avg first candle range is 280 pts — large enough that the option premium (typically 200–600 pts) has room to move significantly on a real breakout. The sustain candle filter (09:20 candle must confirm direction) reduces fake breakouts. This fires once per day, at a very specific time — easy to manage.
✅ Afternoon Momentum (MOM) — The Second Trade Engine
28.3% of all afternoon 5m candles meet the big-body threshold (≥40 pts or ≥0.5×ATR). That's an average of 6.8 qualifying candles per afternoon session — but with EMA9/EMA21 trend confirmation (in the live strategy), most of those are filtered down to 1–2 real signals. On the 40 trending days, 15 of them had the biggest session move in the afternoon — that's 37.5% of trending days where the afternoon was the real money session, not the morning.
⚠ EMA Crossover — Over-Fires, Needs Heavy Filtering
Even with the EMA gap filter (|EMA9−EMA21| ≥ 10 pts), the EMA crossover fires 18.4 signals per day on average — on every single day in the 6 months. That's not a signal, that's noise. The raw EMA strategy without additional filters will churn your capital on the 57% sideways days. EMA is only useful as a trend-confirmation filter for other strategies (ORB sustain, MOM), not as a standalone entry trigger.
❌ VWAP — Dangerous on Sideways Days (Which Are 57% of Days)
On sideways days, price crosses VWAP an average of 9.1 times per day. On the recent bad days (Sep 16–17), it crossed 18 times per day. Any VWAP retest strategy will fire 4–6 times in both directions on these days. The VWAP strategy only makes sense on trending days where it crosses only 4.6 times on average — but you cannot know it's a trending day at 09:15 when you start. If you enable VWAP, add a strict regime filter: only trade VWAP signals if the ORB breakout has already been sustained.
ℹ Hourly Volatility — 09:xx is the Power Hour
The 9 AM hour (09:15–09:59) has the highest avg 5m candle range at 111.5 pts. This drops sharply to 68.5 pts in the 10 AM hour and continues falling to 57–62 pts at midday. It picks up slightly to 67 pts at 14:xx and 75.8 pts at 15:xx (expiry/closing effect). Strategy implication: the biggest move opportunity is in the first 45 minutes. ORB captures exactly this window.

Phase 2 — Add TRB for days where ORB fails
Add TRB. The data shows that on 57% of sideways days, the biggest move still happens somewhere — just not at the 09:15 open. TRB captures the 10:00+ breakout attempt from the 09:45 anchor candle. It only fires on fresh crossovers, so it won't spam on sideways days.
Add PDHL with buffer=0. These breakouts (prev day high/low) are rare but tend to run far. Low noise, high conviction when they fire.
VWAP conditional: only enable if you add regime detection. On days where ORB has already been sustained (trending days), VWAP retest is valid. On days where ORB failed to sustain — disable VWAP for that day.
